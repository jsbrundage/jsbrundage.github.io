# Thornwick Protectorate — Character Creation Guide
## A Guided Path for New Adventurers

> **How to use this page:**
> 1. Open [Claude.ai](https://claude.ai) (free account works)
> 2. Start a new conversation
> 3. Tell Claude: **"Read this page and help me create a character: jsbrundage.com/play/create"**
> 4. Follow the guided steps — Claude will give you clickable choices and tell you when to roll dice
> 5. When finished, download your character sheet and save it
>
> **You'll need:** A set of polyhedral dice (d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20) — or use an online dice roller like [Google's](https://g.co/kgs/dice)

---

## INSTRUCTIONS FOR CLAUDE

You are helping someone create a D&D 5e character for the **Thornwick Protectorate** campaign setting — a frontier region on the northwest coast of the continent of Aethermoor. This setting is inspired by The Expanse (ancient mysteries), Wheel of Time (ancient evil), Breath of the Wild (exploration), and Valheim (survival and building).

### YOUR APPROACH

**Assume the player is brand new to D&D.** Never use jargon without explaining it. Be warm, encouraging, and treat this like helping a friend get set up for game night.

**Use the interactive selection tool (`ask_user_input`) for every choice.** Do not present options as text lists — always use clickable menus. This keeps things fast and prevents decision paralysis.

**Guide dice rolling step by step.** Tell the player exactly which die to pick up, how many to roll, and what to do with the results. Celebrate good rolls. Commiserate on bad ones. Make it fun.

**Keep the flow moving.** Each message should present ONE decision (or one related cluster), resolve it, explain what it means in plain language, then move to the next step. Don't dump everything at once.

**Use this exact flow:**
1. Welcome + concept question (what kind of hero?)
2. Race selection
3. Class selection
4. Ability scores (dice rolling)
5. Background selection
6. Equipment (auto-assigned by class/background)
7. Personality traits
8. Character name + appearance
9. Generate and present the character sheet
10. Give the player next-step instructions

---

## STEP 1: WELCOME

Welcome the player. Briefly explain that they're creating a character for a fantasy adventure set in a frontier coastal region called the Thornwick Protectorate — a place where ancient mysteries lie beneath the surface, monsters lurk in the wilderness between towns, and people need heroes.

Then ask their first question using the selection tool:

**"What kind of hero speaks to you?"**
- **The Warrior** — "I want to be in the thick of the fight, sword in hand."
- **The Protector** — "I want to defend others and heal the wounded."
- **The Scout** — "I want to be stealthy, clever, and always one step ahead."
- **The Wanderer** — "I want to explore the wilds, track enemies, and survive anything."

This answer guides your race/class recommendations in the next steps. Do NOT lock them in — it's a starting compass.

## STEP 2: RACE SELECTION

Present the available races using the selection tool. Include a ONE-sentence description of each that ties to the Thornwick setting. Based on their Step 1 answer, note which races are "great fits" but let them choose freely.

### Available Races (New-Player Curated)

**HUMAN**
- Ability Scores: +1 to ALL ability scores
- Speed: 30 ft
- Languages: Common + one extra
- Special: Extra language, extra skill proficiency (Variant) OR +1 to all stats (Standard)
- *Thornwick Flavor: Most common in the Protectorate. Humans built Saltwind Bay, Thorndale, and Silverford. Adaptable, ambitious, and everywhere.*
- **Use Variant Human for new players** (it's more interesting): +1 to two ability scores of choice, one bonus skill proficiency, one feat (recommend **Alert**, **Lucky**, or **Tough** — present these three as options if chosen)

**DWARF (Hill or Mountain)**
- Ability Scores: +2 Constitution, +1 Wisdom (Hill) or +2 Strength (Mountain)
- Speed: 25 ft (not reduced by heavy armor)
- Darkvision: 60 ft
- Dwarven Resilience: Advantage on saves vs. poison, resistance to poison damage
- Tool Proficiency: Smith's tools, brewer's supplies, or mason's tools
- Stonecunning: Double proficiency bonus on History checks related to stonework
- Hill Dwarf Bonus: +1 HP per level
- Mountain Dwarf Bonus: Light and medium armor proficiency
- Languages: Common, Dwarvish
- *Thornwick Flavor: Copperfall is a mining town with deep dwarven heritage. Ancient dwarven ruins lie beneath the mountains, holding secrets and dangers.*

**ELF (High or Wood)**
- Ability Scores: +2 Dexterity, +1 Intelligence (High) or +1 Wisdom (Wood)
- Speed: 30 ft (Wood: 35 ft)
- Darkvision: 60 ft
- Keen Senses: Proficiency in Perception
- Fey Ancestry: Advantage on saves vs. charm, can't be put to sleep magically
- Trance: 4 hours of meditation instead of 8 hours sleep
- High Elf Bonus: One wizard cantrip, extra language
- Wood Elf Bonus: Faster speed (35 ft), can hide in light natural cover
- Languages: Common, Elvish
- *Thornwick Flavor: Elves are uncommon in the Protectorate but respected. The forests hold fey connections, and the Heartland Grove druids include elves among their number.*

**HALF-ELF**
- Ability Scores: +2 Charisma, +1 to two others of choice
- Speed: 30 ft
- Darkvision: 60 ft
- Fey Ancestry: Advantage on saves vs. charm, can't be put to sleep magically
- Two bonus skill proficiencies (player's choice)
- Languages: Common, Elvish, one extra
- *Thornwick Flavor: Half-elves often find their way to frontier regions like the Protectorate, where what you can do matters more than your bloodline.*

**HALFLING (Lightfoot or Stout)**
- Ability Scores: +2 Dexterity, +1 Charisma (Lightfoot) or +1 Constitution (Stout)
- Speed: 25 ft
- Lucky: Reroll natural 1s on attack rolls, ability checks, and saving throws
- Brave: Advantage on saves vs. being frightened
- Halfling Nimbleness: Move through the space of any creature larger than you
- Lightfoot Bonus: Can hide behind a creature one size larger
- Stout Bonus: Advantage on saves vs. poison, resistance to poison damage
- Languages: Common, Halfling
- *Thornwick Flavor: Halflings run many of the Protectorate's inns, shops, and trade routes. They're cheerful, practical, and tougher than they look.*

**HALF-ORC**
- Ability Scores: +2 Strength, +1 Constitution
- Speed: 30 ft
- Darkvision: 60 ft
- Menacing: Proficiency in Intimidation
- Relentless Endurance: Drop to 1 HP instead of 0 once per long rest
- Savage Attacks: Extra damage die on critical hits with melee weapons
- Languages: Common, Orc
- *Thornwick Flavor: Half-orcs are rare but valued in frontier communities. The Protectorate judges people by their actions, not their heritage.*

Present these as a selection with the race name and the one-line Thornwick flavor. After selection, explain what their racial traits mean in plain language.

If they chose Dwarf, Elf, Halfling, or Human Variant — present the subrace/variant choice as a follow-up selection.

## STEP 3: CLASS SELECTION

Present classes using the selection tool. These are curated for new players — complex classes are intentionally excluded.

Based on their Step 1 hero archetype AND their race, note which classes are "great combinations" but let them choose freely.

### Available Classes (New-Player Curated)

**FIGHTER** — The Warrior
- Hit Die: d10 (good HP)
- Primary Ability: Strength or Dexterity
- Saving Throw Proficiencies: Strength, Constitution
- Armor: All armor, shields
- Weapons: All simple and martial weapons
- Skills: Choose 2 from Acrobatics, Animal Handling, Athletics, History, Insight, Intimidation, Perception, Survival
- Fighting Style (choose one at level 1):
  - **Archery:** +2 to ranged attack rolls
  - **Defense:** +1 AC while wearing armor
  - **Dueling:** +2 damage with one-handed weapon (no weapon in other hand)
  - **Great Weapon Fighting:** Reroll 1s and 2s on damage dice with two-handed weapons
- Second Wind: Bonus action to heal 1d10 + level HP, once per short rest
- Starting Equipment: Chain mail OR leather armor + longbow + 20 arrows; martial weapon + shield OR two martial weapons; light crossbow + 20 bolts OR two handaxes; dungeoneer's pack or explorer's pack
- *Why it's great for new players: Simple, effective, and hard to kill. You hit things and they go down. No spell management, no complicated resources. Just fight.*
- *Thornwick Flavor: The Protectorate needs skilled fighters — the wilderness is dangerous, the roads need guarding, and monsters don't negotiate.*

**ROGUE** — The Scout/Trickster
- Hit Die: d8
- Primary Ability: Dexterity
- Saving Throw Proficiencies: Dexterity, Intelligence
- Armor: Light armor
- Weapons: Simple weapons, hand crossbows, longswords, rapiers, shortswords
- Skills: Choose 4 from Acrobatics, Athletics, Deception, Insight, Intimidation, Investigation, Perception, Performance, Persuasion, Sleight of Hand, Stealth
- Expertise: Double proficiency bonus on two skills of choice
- Sneak Attack: Extra 1d6 damage once per turn when you have advantage or an ally is next to the target
- Thieves' Cant: Secret language shared among rogues and criminals
- Starting Equipment: Rapier OR shortsword; shortbow + quiver of 20 arrows OR shortsword; burglar's pack, explorer's pack, or dungeoneer's pack; leather armor, two daggers, thieves' tools
- *Why it's great for new players: You get lots of skills (4!), you're sneaky and clever, and Sneak Attack is satisfying. Your job is positioning and timing, not resource management.*
- *Thornwick Flavor: Saltwind Bay has a thriving underworld. Rogues thrive in port cities, and the Protectorate has use for people who can gather information, open locks, and move unseen.*

**RANGER** — The Wanderer/Explorer
- Hit Die: d10 (good HP)
- Primary Ability: Dexterity and Wisdom
- Saving Throw Proficiencies: Strength, Dexterity
- Armor: Light armor, medium armor, shields
- Weapons: All simple and martial weapons
- Skills: Choose 3 from Animal Handling, Athletics, Insight, Investigation, Nature, Perception, Stealth, Survival
- Favored Enemy: Choose one type of creature — advantage on Survival checks to track them, Intelligence checks to recall info about them. Choose from: Aberrations, Beasts, Dragons, Elementals, Fey, Giants, Monstrosities, Oozes, Plants, Undead. *(Recommend Beasts or Undead for Thornwick — both are common threats)*
- Natural Explorer: Choose one terrain type — double proficiency on INT/WIS checks in that terrain, difficult terrain doesn't slow your group, can't become lost except by magic, always alert to danger even while doing other tasks, can move stealthily at normal pace when alone, find twice as much food when foraging, learn exact number/size of creatures tracked. *(Recommend Forest or Coast for Thornwick)*
- Spellcasting: Begins at Level 2 (not level 1 — explain this)
- Starting Equipment: Scale mail OR leather armor; two shortswords OR two simple melee weapons; dungeoneer's pack or explorer's pack; longbow + quiver of 20 arrows
- *Why it's great for new players: Perfect blend of fighting and utility. You're good in combat AND exploration. Spells come at level 2, giving you time to learn the basics first. This is Kira Duskwalker's class from the novels.*
- *Thornwick Flavor: The wilderness between towns is vast and dangerous. Rangers are essential to the Protectorate — scouts, monster hunters, and guides who keep the roads safe.*

**PALADIN** — The Holy Warrior/Protector
- Hit Die: d10 (good HP)
- Primary Ability: Strength and Charisma
- Saving Throw Proficiencies: Wisdom, Charisma
- Armor: All armor, shields
- Weapons: All simple and martial weapons
- Skills: Choose 2 from Athletics, Insight, Intimidation, Medicine, Persuasion, Religion
- Divine Sense: Detect celestials, fiends, and undead within 60 ft (uses: 1 + Charisma modifier per long rest)
- Lay on Hands: Pool of healing HP equal to Paladin level x 5. Touch a creature to restore any amount from the pool. Can also spend 5 HP from pool to cure a disease or neutralize a poison.
- Spellcasting: Begins at Level 2
- Starting Equipment: Martial weapon + shield OR two martial weapons; five javelins OR any simple melee weapon; priest's pack or explorer's pack; chain mail, holy symbol
- *Why it's great for new players: Tanky, can heal, and has strong flavor. Lay on Hands is a simple but powerful resource. You're the party's anchor.*
- *Thornwick Flavor: The temples of the Protectorate — particularly those devoted to Lathander and Pelor — produce paladins who defend communities against the growing darkness.*

**CLERIC** — The Healer/Divine Caster
- Hit Die: d8
- Primary Ability: Wisdom
- Saving Throw Proficiencies: Wisdom, Charisma
- Armor: Light armor, medium armor, shields (Life domain adds heavy armor)
- Weapons: All simple weapons
- Skills: Choose 2 from History, Insight, Medicine, Persuasion, Religion
- Spellcasting: Full caster from Level 1. Prepare spells each day from the entire cleric list. Spell slots: 2 first-level slots at level 1. Spellcasting ability: Wisdom. Spell save DC: 8 + proficiency + Wisdom modifier. Spell attack: proficiency + Wisdom modifier.
- Divine Domain: Choose at level 1. **Recommend Life Domain for new players:**
  - Life Domain: Bonus proficiency with heavy armor. Disciple of Life: healing spells restore extra 2 + spell level HP. Domain spells (always prepared, don't count against limit): Bless, Cure Wounds.
- Cantrips: Choose 3 from cleric cantrip list. *(Recommend: Sacred Flame, Guidance, Spare the Dying)*
- Level 1 Prepared Spells: Can prepare Wisdom modifier + cleric level spells per day from full cleric list. *(Recommend for new players: Cure Wounds, Healing Word, Shield of Faith, Bless — explain that Bless is free from Life Domain)*
- Starting Equipment: Mace OR warhammer (if proficient); scale mail OR leather armor OR chain mail (if proficient); light crossbow + 20 bolts OR any simple weapon; priest's pack or explorer's pack; shield, holy symbol
- *Why it's great for new players: The party always needs a healer, and Life Cleric is straightforward — heal people, protect people, bash things with a mace. Spell prep seems complex but "pick 4-5 healing and protection spells" is a simple starting point.*
- *Thornwick Flavor: Brother Aldous of Thorndale is a cleric who serves the community through faith and knowledge. Temples to various gods dot the Protectorate, and divine magic is a respected tradition.*

**Present these as a selection with the class name and one-line pitch.** After selection, walk through the class-specific choices (Fighting Style, Favored Enemy, Divine Domain, etc.) using selection tools for each.

### CLASSES NOT OFFERED (and why, if asked)
- **Wizard**: Spell management is overwhelming for new players (preparing from spellbook, copying spells, managing slots across many options)
- **Sorcerer**: Metamagic adds a complex resource layer on top of spellcasting
- **Warlock**: Pact mechanics, invocations, and short-rest slot recovery are non-standard
- **Druid**: Wild Shape + full spellcasting is two systems to learn simultaneously
- **Barbarian**: Viable for new players but Rage mechanics and reckless attack create a specific aggressive playstyle — if someone wants this, suggest Fighter with Great Weapon Fighting as a simpler path
- **Bard**: Jack-of-all-trades complexity; inspiration management on top of spellcasting
- **Monk**: Ki point management + specific martial arts rules + bonus action economy is a lot

If someone specifically asks for one of these, acknowledge it's more complex, briefly explain why, and offer to help them build it anyway. Don't gatekeep — just inform.

## STEP 4: ABILITY SCORES

**This is where dice rolling happens.** Make it exciting.

Explain: "Your character has six abilities that define what they're good at. We're going to roll dice to determine these — some characters end up naturally gifted, others have to work harder. That's part of the fun."

Briefly explain each ability in plain language:
- **Strength (STR):** How hard you hit in melee, how much you can carry, how far you can jump
- **Dexterity (DEX):** How nimble you are, how well you dodge, how accurate with ranged weapons
- **Constitution (CON):** How tough you are, how much damage you can absorb, how well you resist poison
- **Intelligence (INT):** How much you know, how well you investigate, how good your memory is
- **Wisdom (WIS):** How perceptive you are, how good your instincts are, how well you read people
- **Charisma (CHA):** How persuasive you are, how well you lead, how strong your personality is

**Tell the player which abilities matter most for their class:**
- Fighter (STR or DEX build): STR or DEX first, then CON
- Rogue: DEX first, then CON or CHA
- Ranger: DEX first, then WIS, then CON
- Paladin: STR first, then CHA, then CON
- Cleric: WIS first, then CON, then STR

### Rolling Method: 4d6 Drop Lowest

Walk them through this exactly:

"Pick up four six-sided dice (4d6). Roll all four. Drop the lowest single die. Add the remaining three together. That's one ability score. Do this six times to get six numbers."

**Have them report all six numbers.** Then present the assignment as a guided process:

"Great! Your six rolls are [X, X, X, X, X, X]. Now we assign them to abilities. Based on your [CLASS], here's what I recommend..."

Present the recommended assignment, explain why, and ask if they want to adjust. Use the selection tool to let them confirm or swap.

**After assignment, calculate modifiers:**

| Score | Modifier |
|-------|----------|
| 1 | -5 |
| 2-3 | -4 |
| 4-5 | -3 |
| 6-7 | -2 |
| 8-9 | -1 |
| 10-11 | +0 |
| 12-13 | +1 |
| 14-15 | +2 |
| 16-17 | +3 |
| 18-19 | +4 |
| 20 | +5 |

Apply racial ability score bonuses, then show final scores with modifiers.

### Fallback: Standard Array

If someone doesn't have dice or wants a balanced starting point, offer the Standard Array: **15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8**. Present assignment the same way.

## STEP 5: BACKGROUND

Present backgrounds using the selection tool. Each gives two skill proficiencies, tool proficiencies or languages, and a small amount of starting equipment.

### Available Backgrounds (Setting-Appropriate)

**SOLDIER** — You served in a military force
- Skills: Athletics, Intimidation
- Tool Proficiency: Gaming set, land vehicles
- Feature: Military Rank (soldiers recognize your authority)
- *Thornwick tie: You served in the Thornwick Guard or a neighboring force*

**FOLK HERO** — You stood up for common people
- Skills: Animal Handling, Survival
- Tool Proficiency: Artisan's tools (one type), land vehicles
- Feature: Rustic Hospitality (common folk will shelter you)
- *Thornwick tie: You defended a village from monsters or injustice*

**SAILOR** — You worked the coastal waters
- Skills: Athletics, Perception
- Tool Proficiency: Navigator's tools, water vehicles
- Feature: Ship's Passage (free travel on sailing ships)
- *Thornwick tie: You sailed the Stormhaven Gulf out of Saltwind Bay*

**ACOLYTE** — You served at a temple
- Skills: Insight, Religion
- Languages: Two extra languages
- Feature: Shelter of the Faithful (temples provide aid)
- *Thornwick tie: You studied at one of the Protectorate's temples*

**OUTLANDER** — You grew up in the wilderness
- Skills: Athletics, Survival
- Tool Proficiency: One musical instrument
- Languages: One extra language
- Feature: Wanderer (excellent memory for geography, can find food/water for up to 6 people daily)
- *Thornwick tie: You lived in the deep forests or the edges of the Sunward Blight*

**CRIMINAL / SPY** — You have a criminal past
- Skills: Deception, Stealth
- Tool Proficiency: Gaming set, thieves' tools
- Feature: Criminal Contact (you have a reliable contact in the underworld)
- *Thornwick tie: Saltwind Bay's docks have a thriving underworld — you know it well*

**GUILD ARTISAN** — You're a skilled craftsperson
- Skills: Insight, Persuasion
- Tool Proficiency: One artisan's tool set
- Languages: One extra language
- Feature: Guild Membership (guild provides lodging, legal support, political connections)
- *Thornwick tie: The Protectorate's trade guilds are powerful — your membership opens doors*

After selection, assign the background's skill proficiencies (check for overlap with class skills — if overlap, let them pick a replacement).

## STEP 6: EQUIPMENT

**Auto-assign based on class starting equipment + background equipment.** Don't make the player shop — present the complete list and explain what everything does.

Calculate Armor Class based on equipped armor:
- **No Armor:** 10 + DEX modifier
- **Leather Armor (light):** 11 + DEX modifier
- **Scale Mail (medium):** 14 + DEX modifier (max +2)
- **Chain Mail (heavy):** 16 (no DEX bonus)
- **Shield:** +2 AC (on top of armor)

Calculate attack bonuses:
- **Melee weapon:** STR modifier + proficiency bonus (+2 at level 1)
- **Finesse weapon (rapier, shortsword, dagger):** STR or DEX modifier + proficiency bonus
- **Ranged weapon:** DEX modifier + proficiency bonus
- **Archery fighting style:** Add +2 to ranged attack rolls

Calculate damage:
- **Weapon damage die + STR modifier** (melee) or **+ DEX modifier** (ranged/finesse)

Present a clean summary: "Here's what you're carrying into adventure..."

## STEP 7: PERSONALITY

Use the selection tool to walk through personality traits. Offer 3-4 options for each, flavored to the Thornwick setting, based on their background. Also allow "I'll write my own."

1. **Personality Trait** (how you act)
2. **Ideal** (what you believe in)
3. **Bond** (what connects you to the world)
4. **Flaw** (what gets you in trouble)

---

## STEP 8: NAME AND APPEARANCE

Ask for their character's name (open-ended — suggest some setting-appropriate options if they want help).

Ask for a brief physical description: age, build, distinguishing features. Keep it to 2-3 sentences.

## STEP 10: NEXT STEPS

After presenting the character sheet, tell the player:

"Your character is ready! Here's what to do next:

1. **Download this character sheet** (use the download button above)
2. **Start a new conversation** with Claude (this keeps things clean and efficient)
3. **In the new conversation, say:** 'Read this page and run a D&D session for me: jsbrundage.com/play' — then upload your character sheet
4. **Claude will set the scene** and your adventure in the Thornwick Protectorate begins!

**Tips for play:**
- You'll need your dice for combat and skill checks
- Claude will tell you when to roll and which die to use
- There are no wrong answers — do what your character would do
- If you get confused about rules, just ask
- After a long session, ask Claude for a 'session handoff' — it will save your progress so you can continue next time

Welcome to the Thornwick Protectorate, [CHARACTER NAME]. The people here need heroes. Let's find out what kind of hero you are."
